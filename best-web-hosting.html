    The 2-minute guide

    How to choose the right web hosting (not just the cheapest)

    Web hosting is the battery under your website: when it's good, nobody notices; when it's bad, it's all anyone talks about. The catch is that "best hosting" doesn't exist — only the best hosting for your situation. A cheap shared plan is genuinely the right answer for a hobby blog, and a genuinely wrong one for an online store doing serious revenue. This guide walks you through the few decisions that matter, so the six-question quiz above feels less like a black box and more like a checklist.

    Start with the type of hosting, not the brand

    Every host name above is just a wrapper around one of a few real products:

    • Shared hosting — your site shares a server with many others. Cheap, easy, fine for most small sites; you're limited on resources and performance depends on your neighbours.
    • VPS (virtual private server) — a dedicated slice of a server with guaranteed resources and root-level control. The sweet spot for developers, growing sites and custom software.
    • Cloud hosting — your site runs on pools of servers that can spin up more power on demand. Great for traffic spikes; pricing scales with usage.
    • Managed WordPress hosting — WordPress-specific hosting where the provider handles updates, security, backups, caching and staging. The most expensive tier, worth it mainly when your site earns money.
    • Free static hosting (CDN) — plain HTML and CSS files served free from a global CDN like Cloudflare Pages. No server to manage and no monthly bill; the right tier for simple sites that don't need server-side code.

    Most people — including most small businesses — should be comparing these five, and the quiz above is built around exactly that range.

    The five things that actually matter

    1. Support quality, not just availability. When your site goes down at 11pm, is there a live human who can help? For beginners this beats raw specs.
    2. Speed — faster hosting means a faster site, which means better rankings and fewer visitors bouncing. Modern storage (NVMe/SSD) and caching matter more than marketing buzzwords.
    3. Backups and security. Daily automated backups and free SSL should be table stakes on any plan you pay for.
    4. Room to grow. Can you upgrade without migrating to a new company? Hosting that boxes you into one tier is a trap.
    5. What you pay after the first year. The glowing price in the advert is the intro rate; the renewal is what you actually pay. Budget hosts differ a lot here, which is why we flag it on every card.

    Shared, VPS or managed WordPress: which fits your site

    Here's the most useful shortcut we know:

    • A plain static site built with HTML, CSS or a small framework → free static hosting (Cloudflare Pages serves it free; a budget shared plan if you later want email, forms or a database).
    • A blog, portfolio or brochure site on WordPress or a builder → shared hosting (Hostinger or Verpex for value; Bluehost for the friendliest WordPress onboarding).
    • A business site or store on WordPress that needs speed and reliability more than price → managed WordPress or managed cloud (WP Engine if you want the best managed layer, Cloudways if you want cloud scale without the admin).
    • A custom app, SaaS or API built on Node, Python, PHP or a database → VPS or cloud (DigitalOcean if you run your own servers, Cloudways if you'd rather not).
    • Value-first developer, comfortable managing things → InterServer for locked-in pricing and cheap VPS plans.

    Notice what this list does not say: it never tells you to pick the biggest plan "just in case". Buying headroom you don't need is the most common way people overpay.

    What the sticker price hides

    Three things quietly raise your bill on nearly every cheap plan:

    • Renewal rates. A $3/month first-year price becomes a $10–$12/month renewal. Always check the "renews at" number.
    • Resource limits. "Unlimited" storage often has fair-use caps; cheap plans restrict databases, inodes or CPU minutes.
    • Upsells. Domain privacy, premium backups, higher support tiers and "security" add-ons are sold at checkout. Say no until you actually hit a limit.

    The good news: a wrong pick is rarely permanent. Most hosts migrate WordPress sites free, and moving later is routine. Don't let analysis paralysis replace starting.

    Quick answers to common questions

    What is the difference between shared and VPS hosting?

    Shared hosting runs your site on a server with many others — cheap and simple, but limited on resources and at the mercy of your neighbours. A VPS gives you a dedicated slice of a server with guaranteed resources and real control. Choose shared for small or simple sites; choose VPS for performance, custom software or steady growth.

    Is cheap hosting fine for a small blog?

    Yes. For a personal blog or portfolio, budget shared hosting (Hostinger, HostGator, InterServer or Verpex) is usually more than enough. Just check the renewal price before you commit — intro rates are often half of what you'll pay next year.

    Do I need managed WordPress hosting?

    Only when your WordPress site pays for itself. Managed hosting adds automatic updates, security, staging and faster loading for several times the cost of shared. It's worth it for business sites, online stores and agencies — not for a hobby blog.

    Can I move hosts later if I chose badly?

    Almost always, yes. Moving is routine and most hosts migrate WordPress sites for free. Keep your domain registered separately from your hosting — that single habit makes switching hosts trivial.

    Which hosting should a developer choose?

    If you're comfortable on the command line, a cloud VPS like DigitalOcean gives the most control at a predictable price. If you'd rather not maintain servers yourself, a managed cloud platform like Cloudways delivers the same headroom without the sysadmin work.

    What's the difference between a website builder and web hosting?

    A website builder is a tool that builds and often serves your site; hosting is where your site's files live. Many cheap hosts bundle an easy builder, which is perfect if you're starting without a developer and don't want to touch code.

    Does the host's location matter?

    Yes — distance affects load speed. Data centres closer to your audience load faster, which is why providers like Verpex and Cloudways let you pick a region. For a global audience it matters less; for local customers, choose a nearby data centre when you can.
